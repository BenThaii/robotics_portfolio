UR5e manipulation with MoveIt 2

Project: Five-phase industrial-arm program (i01–i05) on a simulated UR5e, ending in a perception-driven pick-and-place mission. Demo: https://benthaii.github.io/robotics_portfolio/robot-arm/ Scope: Gazebo simulation only. No physical arm was driven.

1. What was built

A five-phase ladder, each phase with a runbook and study doc:

phase content
i01 ros2_control bring-up of UR5e in Gazebo — controllers, joint state
i02 Kinematics with MoveIt 2 — planning, IK/FK
i03 Scripted missions — programmed motion sequences
i04 RGB-D perception for the arm
i05 Pick-and-place mission — sense → plan → grasp → place

The mission ran twice in the live sim, both times end to end.

2. Stack

ROS 2 Jazzy · MoveIt 2 · ros2_control · Gazebo (gz sim) · UR5e URDF · RGB-D perception (camera + depth pipeline) · YOLO-segmentation target mask.

3. Perception → planning → grasp

The grasp target comes from the camera, not a hardcoded pose:

Gazebo camera frame
  → YOLO detection box + segmentation mask
  → mask becomes a MoveIt planning-scene collision object
  → planner respects table, target and gripper
  → grasp goal handed to MoveIt
  → execute on ros2_control joint trajectory controllers

MoveIt 2 planning-scene collision objects are added and updated at runtime, so plans respect the changing scene rather than a static world model.

4. Result

OBSERVED from the live mission run:

  • Camera → plan → grasp → place completed end to end
  • 1 camera-derived grasp target — never hardcoded
  • Collision objects added to the planning scene at runtime

The perception figure shows the live run: camera frame with detection box, predicted mask, and the blend; the mask becomes the planning-scene object the arm plans around and grasps.

5. Engineering rigour

Same predict-then-run discipline as the mobile-robot track: runbooks state expected observations, and the six live findings record defects and behaviours with root causes rather than silent patches. Acceptance was a live-tested PASS, not code-review-only completion.

6. Honest scope

  • Simulation only — every result in this track runs in Gazebo against the simulated UR5e; no physical-robot operation is claimed.
  • i01–i05 prove pipeline competence in manipulation simulation, not industrial deployment experience.
  • No invented metrics: the six findings, PASS ×2 and stage counts are the verified record. No success rates or accuracy numbers without a source.
  • Self-directed learning program — no employer, title or dates attached. AI-assisted development: designed and verified by the author, executed with AI coding tools.
